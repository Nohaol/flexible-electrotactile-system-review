# Fig. 2 draft Figure Caption

**Fig. 2 | Physiological mechanisms of natural tactile and electrotactile perception.** **(A)** Natural touch begins with mechanical deformation of the skin and mechanotransduction by cutaneous low-threshold mechanoreceptive afferents; afferent activity is subsequently processed by spinal and supraspinal circuits. The depicted endings are representative and do not imply the same receptor composition in glabrous and hairy skin. **(B)** In transcutaneous electrotactile stimulation, a programmed electrical pulse is delivered through the electrode–skin interface and establishes an extracellular field in underlying tissue. Electrical excitation can arise through polarization of excitable neural membranes without preceding mechanical deformation; the first activated site and fiber-subtype recruitment depend on electrode, field and neural geometry and are not assigned here. **(C)** Electrical measurements of delivered current and voltage, neural excitation models, and human psychophysical reports probe different levels of this pathway. A reported percept alone does not identify the recruited afferent subtype. **(D)** Pulse amplitude, pulse width and frequency alter the electrical input and can affect detection, perceived intensity and upper perceptual endpoints under specified experimental conditions. These endpoints are not interchangeable: a decrease in peak-current threshold with longer pulses does not by itself establish greater comfort, lower delivered charge or a universal safety limit. All shapes, field lines and arrows are conceptual, not to scale, and contain no reproduced source artwork or replotted third-party data.
