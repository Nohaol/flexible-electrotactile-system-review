# Fig. 3 | Electrode–skin interface and equivalent models

**Draft English figure caption.** **Fig. 3 | The electrode–skin interface mediates the transfer from a programmed stimulus to a variable tissue input.** **a,** Conceptual path from pulse driver through the electrode, contact layer, stratum corneum and viable tissue. The return electrode and true spatial field are omitted for clarity; the dashed connection to an excitable membrane denotes a mechanistic inference, not identified fiber recruitment. **b,** Condition-specific circuit topologies. For a dry metal recording electrode, a contact capacitive/CPE branch is in series with the parallel stratum-corneum resistance and capacitive/CPE branch and deeper-tissue resistance [Goyal et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22218510). A pre-gelled Ag/AgCl recording-electrode model resolves electrode charge-transfer resistance and double-layer capacitance, gel resistance, epidermal RC behavior and subcutaneous resistance [Murphy et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21155210). A hydrogel transcutaneous-stimulation model uses a series resistance and a parallel RC branch whose effective conductance changes with current and accumulated charge [Vargas Luna et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0125609). Here `||` indicates a parallel branch, and CPE denotes a constant-phase element; these circuits are alternative models for distinct interfaces, not interchangeable descriptions. **c,** Small-signal EIS characterizes frequency-dependent terminal response, whereas stimulation-pulse measurements follow time-dependent current, voltage and phase charge. Curves are original schematic traces, not reconstructed experimental data. Their separation reflects the wide-band skin measurements of [Rosell et al., 1988](https://doi.org/10.1109/10.4599) and the pulse-response analysis of [Vargas Luna et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0125609). **d,** Hydration, electrode area and material, and wear time changed interface measurements in the cited recording studies. Contact pressure and motion are plausible additional perturbations but were not independently quantified in those studies. The dashed link from terminal electrical response to neural recruitment and perception indicates that a separate neural or psychophysical measurement is needed. The control implication is informed by the impedance-feedback demonstration of [Kajimoto, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1109/TOH.2011.39), whose full text has not yet been verified. The figure is an original synthesis; it reproduces no published image or numerical dataset.
